# Something they already have

A person who has already sketched, mocked, or prototyped what they want should
be built toward it, rather than interviewed blind or shown a throwaway that
rediscovers a decision they had already made.

Before building a decision prototype, ask whether they already have something.
Where they do, it settles the question and the throwaway is not built.

## What counts

A mock or design, whether an image or a design tool. A clickable prototype. A
screenshot of another product, meaning "like this". A hand sketch or a
whiteboard photo. A written brief. The spreadsheet that is the current manual
tool. A sample data file that shows the shape of the data. An example of the
output wanted. A reference link. A recording of the flow they want.

A file in the recorded design tool may be opened directly where the harness can
reach it. Where it cannot, the ordinary screenshot or description route still
applies.

## Say back what you see

Read it, then say what you found with your guesses attached, the way an
interview question carries its guess: "I see a list with add, edit, and a search
box, and deleting has no confirm step. Is that right?" Correcting a wrong
reading is easier than answering a blank.

Where the harness cannot open the image or the link, say so and ask them to
describe it or send a screenshot. Never pretend to have seen it. The person
should not have to know which coding agent can read pictures.

## What it settles, and what it never does

It settles look and behaviour. It never settles the build path. A mock showing
outside sign-in, payments, or stored personal information goes through the fit
check and earns the risk notice exactly as the same request typed in words
would. An artifact cannot carry work past a check.

An artifact is a design rather than production code. Where they bring a coded
prototype, its code stays throwaway: pinned down by tests or rebuilt cleanly.
Genuinely working code is a different route, `adopting.md`.

Never take real data or a secret out of an artifact. A screenshot full of real
customer records shows the shape of the data and never becomes a fixture of real
data, and a password visible in a picture is neither stored nor repeated back.

## Cut it down

A mock usually shows more than one piece. Cut it into pieces during founding, or
build only the piece in hand. Never build the whole mock in one go.

Name what the artifact does not cover rather than inventing it: the missing
screens, the empty states, the errors, who may do what. Those are asked or
parked. A mock is rarely a complete description.

Where two artifacts disagree, or an artifact contradicts the masterplan, put the
conflict to the person and let them choose. Never resolve it quietly. A loose
reference, such as "make it feel like this other product", is turned into
concrete behaviours anybody could check, and whatever cannot be turned into one
is parked rather than promised.

## Record the decision

Fill the same record a decision prototype fills: the question, what was decided,
and what the production build must preserve. Write it in words, in the
masterplan at founding or on the piece afterwards.

The artifact itself may live outside the project, as a link or an image, and a
link that breaks later must not take the decision with it. Record the decision
rather than the pixels.
