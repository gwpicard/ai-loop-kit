# Prototype: does this behave the way I meant

The person drives it. They press things, watch what happens, and tell you where
it does not match how the work really goes. Nothing here is described to them.

## What to build

One file they open by double-clicking, that needs nothing installed and nothing
running. The person who knows the work best is often not the person building it,
and they will not set anything up. One file can be sent to them, opened, and
driven on their own machine, which is what makes their answer worth having.

That rules out reaching for the parts the real project is built from. Those need
setting up, and setting up is exactly what stops this reaching the person who
should see it.

## What it has to do

Label everything in the words of their work, not the words of the build. A
button says what it does for them.

Show the current state after every action, in plain sight. Somebody deciding
whether a flow is right has to see what the flow just did. A thing that changes
something invisible teaches nobody anything.

Offer a guided path through the awkward cases, not only free play. Name the
cases in their own words and walk them: the booking that clashes, the form left
half finished, the person without permission, the same action twice. A screen of
buttons and no suggestion of where to start is a screen nobody presses, and it
costs a whole build to find that out.

Use invented data that looks like theirs. Enough of it that the awkward cases
can actually happen.

## What to bring back

The decision, in one line, and what the production build must keep. Where they
corrected how the work really goes, that correction is worth more than the
prototype and goes into the record rather than staying in the conversation.
